Outfits page

A hero outfit, tied to Shuffle

One big outfit at the top of both Outfits pages, driven by the Shuffle button, with the grid of others below it. Then four ways to make the unfiltered All page worth opening. Every phone below is live. Tap Shuffle, the locks and the chips, and scroll inside the phone.

Give every deal a reasonOne line above the hero says why you are looking at it. Random with a reason reads as a suggestion.
Control without effortLocking a piece keeps what you love and rerolls the rest. One tap, no filter sheet.
Start from what you trustYour faves and your newest pieces are better starting points than a blank shuffle.
Always a way backShuffle can lose an outfit you liked. An undo keeps it safe to keep tapping.
My pick

Locks, a Shuffle bar and lenses

Layout C below, combined with the Lenses from Discovery 1. All gets lenses that explain the deal. Faves gets the fave levels you already have, and Shuffle picks among outfits you already trust. When the hero scrolls away, Shuffle floats back as the wand you have now.

Why this oneThe hero is the one place that answers "what do I wear?". Shuffle sits directly under it, so cause and effect are next to each other.
LocksTap a padlock on any piece, then Shuffle. Only the unlocked pieces change. This is the fastest way to ask "what goes with this jacket?".
LensesSurprise me is the default and works like today. The others give the unfiltered page a purpose. Filters still sit in their pill for precise control.
FavesNo locks there, because a fave is a finished outfit. Shuffle just picks another one, and the chips narrow it by fave level.

Scroll the phone down to see the grid, and the floating Shuffle returns once the hero is out of view.

Part one

How the hero and Shuffle fit together

Three ways to attach Shuffle to the hero. Each is shown on All and on Faves. On Faves, Shuffle picks another outfit you have kept.

Layout A

Shuffle bar under the hero

The simplest change. The hero is large, a wide Shuffle button sits directly under it, and an undo arrow sits beside it.

GoodBiggest tap target on the page, right under your thumb's resting place. Undo makes it safe to keep dealing.
Trade-offTakes a row of height from the grid. Nothing lets you hold a piece you like.
Layout B

Card deck with a corner wand

The hero is the top of a deck. Swipe it away or tap the wand on its corner. Cards behind it say there is more. Try dragging the card sideways.

GoodMost playful. Swiping is the natural gesture for "next", and the wand badge keeps your existing Shuffle identity.
Trade-offSwipe is hidden, so the wand has to do the teaching. A sideways swipe also competes with the chip row if lenses are added.
Layout C

Lockable pieces

Layout A plus a padlock on each piece. Locked pieces stay put when you Shuffle, like holds on a slot machine. On Faves the locks are not offered.

GoodTurns Shuffle from a lottery into a conversation: keep the piece you want to wear, reroll the rest. This is where the creativity is.
Trade-offFour small padlocks on the card. They need a first-use hint: the line under Shuffle covers it.
Part two

Making unfiltered All worth opening

With no filters, All is a stream of random combinations with no reason to look at any one of them. The value is finding new ways to wear what you own, so each option below gives the stream a point of view. They only use what the app already stores: fave levels, when a piece was added, and which pieces appear in your faves. None of them needs wear tracking.

Discovery 1

Lenses

A row of chips under the title. Each one changes how the hero and the grid are dealt, and the line above the hero says what you are looking at.

New inOutfits built around pieces you added recently, so a new purchase gets worn with everything else.
OverlookedBuilt around pieces that are in none of your faves. The point is to give them a chance.
Remix a faveOne of your faves with a single piece swapped. Familiar, with one new idea.
Trade-offFour chips is the limit before it becomes a menu. Surprise me stays first, so the page still works with zero decisions.
Discovery 2

Shelves

With no filters, the page below the hero becomes a few labelled shelves you scroll sideways, then the open grid. Turn a filter on and it collapses back to the plain grid.

GoodBrowsing, not choosing. You see three kinds of idea at once with no taps, the way a streaming app shows rows.
Trade-offMore to build. Shelves also dilute Shuffle: it rerolls the hero and every shelf, which is a lot to change at once.
Fits withLenses. The shelf titles are the same ideas, and "See all" can open that lens.
Discovery 3

Start from a piece

A strip of your pieces under the title. Tap one and every outfit is built around it, hero and grid. Tap it again, or Anything, to go back.

GoodAnswers the most common real question: "what do I wear with this?". It is also the best way to use something you forgot you owned.
Trade-offNeeds a decision from you, so it is a poor default. Works best as a fifth lens that reveals the strip, rather than a bar that is always open.
Discovery 4

Remix a fave

The hero is one of your faves with a single piece swapped. The new piece is ringed, and the one it replaced sits in its corner. Shuffle swaps something else. Keep it with the heart.

GoodLow risk, because 7 of 8 pieces are things you already trust. A tiny change is easy to judge, and it teaches you which swaps work.
Trade-offNeeds faves to exist, so a new wardrobe falls back to Surprise me. It also only finds neighbours of what you already like.
What each needs

Built from data the app already has

Hero and ShuffleThe app already has a hero card and a Shuffle that rerolls it. Layouts A to C change where the button lives and add holds. The hero pick is already kept for the visit.
New inUses each piece's created date. No new column.
Overlooked and RemixUses saved outfits and fave levels. No new column.
Not possible yet"Haven't worn lately" needs wear tracking, which does not exist. It would be the strongest lens, so it is worth a separate conversation.
